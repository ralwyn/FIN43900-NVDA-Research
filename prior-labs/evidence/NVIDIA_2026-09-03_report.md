# NVIDIA: Exceptional Execution, Expectations Still Unpriced

**Company:** NVIDIA Corporation (NASDAQ: NVDA)  
**Report date:** September 3, 2026  
**Decision user:** Equity-research analyst considering whether to initiate coverage  
**Current call:** **Watch–defer**

## Executive view

NVIDIA has become the central computing-platform supplier for the current buildout of artificial-intelligence infrastructure. The operating evidence is unusually strong: revenue expanded from $60.9 billion in fiscal 2024 to $130.5 billion in fiscal 2025 and $215.9 billion in fiscal 2026, while fiscal Q2 2027 revenue reached $96.2 billion. Yet a strong company is not automatically an attractive security. Customer concentration, export restrictions, custom accelerators, rapid product transitions, and the possibility that hyperscalers fail to earn adequate returns on AI capital spending could make current growth and margins less durable than recent results imply.

I would admit NVIDIA to detailed valuation research but **wait for a complete intrinsic-value model before initiating a buy**. The evidence supports the business; it does not yet establish a sufficient margin of safety in the stock price.

## What the company is

NVIDIA is a full-stack accelerated-computing company. Its economic proposition extends beyond GPUs to networking, systems, software, libraries, and developer tools. Its principal markets are Data Center, Gaming, Professional Visualization, and Automotive, but Data Center is now the dominant growth engine. NVIDIA's platform breadth and installed software ecosystem can reduce customer switching incentives and reinforce demand for successive hardware generations.

## What is going well

### Growth remains extraordinary

NVIDIA's fiscal 2024 revenue was $60.9 billion. It rose 114% to $130.5 billion in fiscal 2025 and then another 65% to $215.9 billion in fiscal 2026. Operating income reached $130.4 billion in fiscal 2026, up 60% year over year. These results show that growth has translated into operating profit rather than remaining only a demand narrative. [FY2024 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581024000029/nvda-20240128.htm), [FY2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581025000023/nvda-20250126.htm), [FY2026 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000021/nvda-20260125.htm)

Momentum continued after the annual filing. Fiscal Q2 2027 revenue was $96.2 billion, up 106% from a year earlier, and Data Center revenue was $89.0 billion, up 117%. GAAP gross margin was 75.0%. These are company-reported figures and still require comparison with later SEC filings, but they indicate that demand had not slowed by July 2026. [NVIDIA fiscal Q2 2027 results](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-financial-results-for-second-quarter-fiscal-2027)

### Cash generation and balance-sheet capacity are strong

Fiscal 2026 operating cash flow was $102.7 billion. NVIDIA spent $6.1 billion on capital expenditures and ended the year with $62.6 billion of cash, cash equivalents, and marketable securities versus $8.5 billion of debt at carrying value. This liquidity gives the company flexibility to fund research, supply commitments, ecosystem investments, and shareholder returns. [FY2026 Form 10-K, Liquidity and Capital Resources](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000021/nvda-20260125.htm)

### The platform has competitive advantages

NVIDIA combines processors, interconnects, systems, and software rather than selling an isolated chip. That integration can improve time to deployment and performance at data-center scale. The company's rapid product cadence, developer ecosystem, and scale of research spending strengthen its position, although none makes the position permanent.

## What is concerning

### Customer economics may become the constraint

NVIDIA's growth ultimately depends on its customers earning acceptable returns from AI infrastructure. If AI workloads do not generate enough incremental revenue or cost savings, hyperscalers and other customers may slow capital spending. The key falsification question is whether customers can establish a profitable, cash-generative return before custom ASICs and competing merchant accelerators permanently compress NVIDIA's Data Center margins.

### Customer concentration is material

In fiscal 2026, one direct customer represented 22% of revenue and another represented 14%. Concentration can accelerate growth when a few customers expand rapidly, but it also increases exposure to their budgets, bargaining power, internal-chip programs, and deployment timing. [FY2026 Form 10-K, customer concentration disclosure](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000021/nvda-20260125.htm)

### Gross margin already shows transition risk

Fiscal 2026 gross margin declined from 75.0% to 71.1%. NVIDIA attributed the decline partly to its transition from Hopper HGX systems to full-scale Blackwell data-center solutions and to a $4.5 billion H20 inventory and purchase-obligation charge. Total provisions for inventory and excess purchase obligations were $7.2 billion. The business remained highly profitable, but these figures demonstrate that export controls and product transitions can impose real costs. [FY2026 Form 10-K, Results of Operations](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000021/nvda-20260125.htm)

### Competition can attack both share and economics

NVIDIA competes with other accelerator vendors and with custom chips developed by major customers. Alternatives do not need to replace NVIDIA across every workload to matter; they can pressure pricing or capture selected high-volume use cases. A durable forecast therefore cannot assume that recent market share and gross margins persist indefinitely.

The history shows both scale and deceleration: annual revenue growth remained exceptional but slowed from 114% in fiscal 2025 to 65% in fiscal 2026. At the same time, the latest quarterly release reported another acceleration in year-over-year growth. A valuation should not extrapolate either observation mechanically; it should model scenarios for the duration and profitability of AI infrastructure spending. ## Outlook and unresolved questions

Management describes accelerating demand for AI factories, and fiscal Q2 2027 results support that near-term view. Still, management's outlook is not independent proof of long-run value. The next stage of research should answer:

1. What return on invested capital are NVIDIA's largest customers earning from AI infrastructure?
2. How quickly could custom ASICs take economically important workloads?
3. What normalized revenue growth and gross margin are defensible after the buildout phase?
4. How should export restrictions, customer concentration, supply commitments, and product-transition charges affect downside scenarios?
5. What dilution should be expected from stock-based compensation?
6. At the September 3, 2026 market price, what growth and margin path is already embedded in the valuation?

## Current decision

**Watch–defer: wait for more information before making an investment decision.** NVIDIA's business quality and financial momentum justify full valuation work, but this report does not yet compare intrinsic value with the market price. I would change the call to **initiate** if a source-reconciled FCFF model supports material upside under conservative assumptions and customer evidence demonstrates attractive AI-infrastructure returns. I would change it to **do not initiate** if hyperscaler returns disappoint, custom accelerators cause lasting share or margin pressure, or the market price requires implausibly persistent growth.
